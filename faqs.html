Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about eligibility, support, dates, and applying. Still have a question? Reach out to the COAST team.

Applicants must be U.S. citizens, U.S. nationals, or U.S. permanent residents who are enrolled in an undergraduate degree program at the time of the summer experience. Students who will graduate before the program begins are not eligible. Students at all stages are encouraged to apply, including rising sophomores and community-college students.

No. Prior research experience is not required. COAST actively welcomes earlier-stage undergraduates and students from institutions where research opportunities are limited. Selection emphasizes motivation, curiosity, and potential for growth as a researcher, alongside preparation appropriate to the proposed project.

COAST is a fully supported, in-person experience. Participants receive the NSF stipend of $700 per week across all ten weeks, shared cohort housing near the Rosenstiel campus on Virginia Key, and reimbursement for round-trip travel to and from Miami. The weekly stipend is set to cover day-to-day living costs, including meals, so no separate meal allowance is provided. There is no cost to participate.

Students live together in shared housing near the Rosenstiel campus on Virginia Key, adjacent to the labs and field sites where most research takes place, so there is no daily inter-campus commute. Cohort field activities are arranged and transported by the program.

The program runs for ten weeks each summer, roughly mid-May through mid-July, beginning with an orientation week and ending with a culminating public research symposium. For Year 1, the program runs in Summer 2028; subsequent cohorts run in Summers 2029 and 2030. Exact dates will be confirmed upon NSF award.

All applications are submitted through the NSF Education & Training Application (ETAP) platform. There is no application fee. You will provide a personal statement, an academic transcript (unofficial accepted), one faculty reference, and your ranked preferences among the COAST projects.

See the How to Apply page for the full process and key dates.

For the Year 1 (Summer 2028) cohort, applications open in October 2027 and close in February 2028, with review and selection in February–March. Each cohort follows a similar timeline, with applications opening the preceding October. Confirmed dates will be posted here once the award is finalized.

Applicants rank their preferred projects from the COAST pool. The PI, Co-PI, Coordinator, and a rotating mentor subcommittee review applications using a structured rubric and match students to projects based on interests, preparation, and mentor availability. No more than 10 of the 16 projects are active in a given summer, chosen to balance the four themes and five participating departments.

Projects span the coupled coastal Earth system across four themes: Climate, Hazards, and Resilience; Coastal Ecosystems, Restoration, and Health; Biogeochemistry and Environmental Change; and Modeling, AI, and Technology. Work ranges from field and laboratory observation to genomics, isotope geochemistry, climate modeling, and explainable AI. See the Research page for all sixteen projects.

COAST is committed to a safe, respectful, inclusive, and harassment-free environment for all participants.

The program is led by PI Guoqing Lin (Associate Dean of Research, Rosenstiel; Professor, Marine Geosciences) and Co-PI Ben Kirtman (Dean, Rosenstiel; Director, CIMAS). Contact details will be posted here ahead of the first application cycle.
