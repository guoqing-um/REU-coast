Applications

How to Apply

Applications are submitted through NSF ETAP. There is no application fee, and prior research experience is not required.

Applications are received through the NSF Education & Training Application (ETAP) platform. There is no application fee. Prior research experience is not required: COAST actively welcomes earlier-stage undergraduates, including rising sophomores and community-college transfer students.

Apply through NSF ETAP

Confirm eligibility

U.S. citizens, nationals, or permanent residents currently enrolled in an undergraduate degree program. Students at all stages are encouraged to apply.

Prepare your materials

Personal statement, academic transcript (unofficial accepted), one faculty reference, and your ranked preferences among the COAST mentor projects.

Submit through NSF ETAP

All applications are managed through the NSF ETAP portal. Submitting through ETAP also supports longitudinal tracking of REU participants over time.

Project matching

The PI, Co-PI, Coordinator, and a rotating mentor subcommittee review applications using a structured rubric. Final offers and project matches are made in April (Year 1) or in late winter / early spring (Years 2 and 3), with alternates identified.

Arrive on Virginia Key

Cohort programming begins in June with an orientation week and a campus tour of Rosenstiel and CIMAS.

Eligibility & Selection Criteria

Per NSF requirements, applicants must be U.S. citizens, nationals, or permanent residents enrolled in an undergraduate degree program at the time of the summer experience. Students who will graduate before the program starts are not eligible.

Selection emphasizes:

  • Motivation and curiosity
  • Preparation appropriate to the proposed project
  • Potential for growth as a researcher
  • Alignment with available mentor projects
  • Evidence of persistence, problem-solving, or teamwork

At least half of each cohort is drawn from academic institutions where undergraduate research opportunities are limited, including primarily undergraduate institutions and community colleges. Experiential, mentored research is part of the Rosenstiel School’s essence, and COAST is built to open that experience to students whose home colleges do not offer it.
