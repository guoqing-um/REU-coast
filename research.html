Domains & Projects

Research Domains & Projects

Sixteen projects led by twenty faculty mentors, organized in three science domains and joined by a cross-cutting Modeling, AI, and Technology layer that runs through all three.

You will own one project for the summer, scoped so you can carry it from question to finished result in ten weeks. These sixteen projects are the COAST pool; up to ten run in any given summer, and you rank your preferences among the projects offered that year.

1

Coastal Hazards and Climate Extremes

6 projects  ·  ATM · EVR · MBE · MGS · OCE

These projects follow climate forcing and extreme events from source to consequence, and ask how well communities and ecosystems absorb them: from marine heatwaves and floods to the storm and ocean energy entering the solid Earth.

M. Arcodia ATM

Explainable AI for prediction of coastal marine heatwaves in the Caribbean, Florida, and Gulf region. You train an interpretable model for one sub-region and work out which physical predictors are driving it.

Anchors the Modeling, AI & Technology layer.

P. Zuidema ATM

The Florida Atmospheric Boundary Layer Experiment (FABLE) profiles temperature, humidity, winds, and aerosols along a southeast Florida transect. You quality-control the profiles from one site, then composite and visualize the daily cycle.

Draws on: atmospheric observing technology.

K. Mach EVR & A. Clement ATM

Climate-risk and adaptation decision science, working where physical climate analysis meets the flood and heat adaptation choices South Florida governments are already making. You compute one community-scale heat metric, or evaluate flood-model output under one adaptation scenario, and write a short memo for the partners who will use it.

Bridges to: Domain 2 (community heat exposure and health).

D. Lirman MBE

Coral-reef restoration performance on Florida's Coral Reef, which has lost most of its living coral to warming, disease, and the 2023 marine heatwave. Working with the lab at the Southeast Florida Coral Reef Restoration Hub, you curate one outplant-monitoring dataset or run one experiment on how restored corals perform.

Bridges to: Domain 2 (reef ecosystems and living coasts).

G. Lin MGS

A dense-node seismic array across South Florida images shallow carbonate-platform structure and ocean-storm-to-solid-Earth coupling. You cross-correlate a subset of stations using ambient-noise methods and estimate shear-wave speeds, or characterize the microseism signal itself.

Draws on: dense-node arrays as autonomous observing technology.

F. P. Tuchen OCE

How equatorial and tropical Atlantic currents shape South Florida climate, using long-term moored observations. You quality-control one moored current record and relate it to a climate index and to Atlantic heat transport.

Bridges to: Domain 3 (long-term record of change).

2

Living Coasts: Ecosystems, Organisms, and Human Health

4 projects  ·  EVR · MBE · OCE

These projects examine how coastal ecosystems, organisms, and communities respond to environmental change, pairing field, experimental, and genomic data with quantitative analysis of ecological and human-health outcomes.

K. Ashad-Bishop EVR

Environmental health in Miami-Dade County, one of the most heat-exposed urban coasts in the nation. For most students this is a first encounter with data about people rather than the ocean. You will frame one question on urban-heat exposure or access to coastal resources, working with existing anonymized data.

Bridges to: Domain 1 (extreme heat as a climate hazard).

D. Crawford & M. Oleksiak MBE

Population genomics of fish and invertebrate species in Biscayne Bay, a rapidly changing urban estuary. The work runs at the bench and in code, which makes it a good first exposure to both: you take one species from DNA extraction through to a population-genomic comparison of two contrasting sites.

Draws on: computational genomics and bioinformatics.

R. Heuer MBE

Effects of climate stressors and environmental pollutants on neural and behavioral function in marine fish and invertebrates. You will test one stressor against a single neural or behavioral endpoint.

Bridges to: Domain 1 (climate stressors) and Domain 3 (pollutant biogeochemistry).

H. Close OCE

Isotopic tracers of the ocean's biological pump, using marine particulate matter, sediments, or organism tissues. You reduce one set of isotopic samples and interpret what it says about carbon and nitrogen sources or food-web structure.

Bridges to: Domain 3 (biogeochemical cycling).

3

Coastal Change and Earth-System Cycles

6 projects  ·  ATM · MBE · MGS · OCE

These projects reconstruct long-term change and the cycles that drive it, reducing a sediment core, metagenome, or autonomous-float record to a focused measurement, or using computational models of ocean, atmosphere, and solid-Earth processes.

S. Wang ATM

A machine-learning emulator of an aerosol chemistry model, built to run far faster than the full model. You train an emulator over a defined range of aerosol compositions and benchmark it against the full model.

Anchors the Modeling, AI & Technology layer.

E. Babcock MBE

Quantitative marine ecology applied to fisheries and ecosystem management. You model one question in R: protected-species bycatch, species distribution, or the value of seagrass restoration for juvenile gag grouper. You will learn the modeling as you go.

Draws on: the Modeling, AI & Technology layer.

M. Moniruzzaman MBE

Giant viruses, surprisingly complex microbes that shape coastal food webs and biogeochemistry by infecting algae and other plankton. Working from metagenomes the lab has already generated, you annotate one viral marker group across a single environmental contrast.

Bridges to: Domain 2 (microbial ecology).

G. Eberli, A. Oehlert & R. Weger MGS

An exceptional, high-recovery Last-Glacial-Maximum reef core records deglacial sea-level rise with rare fidelity, a direct analog for the rise now reshaping South Florida's carbonate coast. You own one interval of that core, from making the measurements to placing it on the deglacial sea-level curve.

Bridges to: Domain 1 (paleo record of sea-level hazard).

A. Holt MGS

Computational geodynamics of active plate margins and their role in long-term coastal uplift and hazard. You use global plate-reconstruction tools to find double-subduction settings, then test plate-speed scaling relationships in Python.

Bridges to: Domain 1 (long-term tectonic hazard).

M. Bif OCE

A global fleet of autonomous BGC-Argo floats now profiles ocean biogeochemistry continuously. You start by learning what the sensors measure and how they drift, then combine float, satellite, and shipboard data for one region to pin down carbon export or remineralization, uncertainty and all.

Draws on: autonomous floats and remote sensing.

Modeling, AI, and Technology: a layer across all domains

Every student writes code and learns to say what a result actually means physically, and where it breaks down.

Who would you work with?

Every mentor is a working scientist across the five Rosenstiel departments, and you will be part of their group for the summer.
