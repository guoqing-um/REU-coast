Mentors & Projects

Research Domains & Projects

Sixteen faculty projects, organized in three science domains joined by a cross-cutting modeling, AI, and technology layer, that together span the coupled coastal Earth system.

COAST organizes projects into three coastal Earth-system science domains, joined by a cross-cutting Modeling, AI, and Technology layer that supplies the shared tools every domain uses. Each domain draws faculty from several of the five participating departments. The full three-year pool comprises 16 projects across the three domains; no more than 10 are active in any single summer, matched to the cohort of 10 students. The active set is chosen each year to balance the three domains and the five participating departments, so the rotating pool preserves COAST's school-wide interdisciplinary signature. Every project sits within the same process arc, from the drivers of change through observed change, mechanisms, and impacts to response and adaptation, so a student on any project can follow the logic of the others. Each project is scoped to a tractable question, a curated dataset or experimental subset, and a well-bounded analytical or experimental move suitable for completion within ten weeks.

1

Coastal Hazards and Climate Extremes

MGS · ATM · EVR · OCE · MBE

How climate forcing and extreme events, from marine heatwaves and floods to the storm and ocean energy entering the solid Earth, propagate through the coastal system, and how communities and ecosystems anticipate, withstand, and adapt (MGS, ATM, EVR, OCE, MBE). The six projects share south Florida as a common laboratory and a common analytical arc: quality-controlled observations reduced to focused composites or contrasts and interpreted against one driver such as heat, storm forcing, or ocean energy.

G. Lin MGS

A regional dense nodal seismic array across south Florida investigates shallow carbonate-platform structure and ocean-storm-solid-Earth coupling through the microseism wavefield. The student analyzes a defined station subset using ambient-noise cross-correlation and produces first-order shallow shear-wave dispersion estimates or a microseism characterization.

Draws on: the Modeling, AI & Technology layer (dense-node array as autonomous observing technology).

M. Arcodia ATM

Explainable AI for prediction of coastal marine heatwaves in the Caribbean-Florida-Gulf region, using climate-model output and reanalysis data. The student trains an interpretable model for one sub-region and identifies the dominant physical predictors driving its performance.

Anchors the Modeling, AI & Technology layer; connects to D2 (downstream ecosystem impacts).

P. Zuidema ATM

The Florida Atmospheric Boundary Layer Experiment (FABLE) documents vertical profiles of temperature, humidity, winds, and aerosols along a southeast Florida transect. The student works with a curated profile subset from one site and applies quality control, daily-cycle compositing, and visualization over a targeted period.

Draws on: the Modeling, AI & Technology layer (atmospheric observing technology).

K. Mach EVR & A. Clement ATM

Climate risk and adaptation science in partnership with local governments and community organizations. The student translates chronic-heat datasets into one decision-relevant community metric, or evaluates flood-model output for one adaptation scenario, producing a partner-oriented memo alongside the standard product package.

Bridges to: D2 (community health, heat exposure, and human-environment vulnerability).

F. P. Tuchen OCE

How equatorial and tropical Atlantic ocean currents shape South Florida climate, using long-term moored current observations. The student quality-controls and analyzes one moored current time series and relates it to a climate index and western-Atlantic heat transport.

Bridges to: D3 (long-term oceanographic record of change).

D. Lirman MBE

Coral-reef restoration research integrating field, laboratory, and citizen-science components. The student analyzes a defined monitoring dataset or runs a small experiment on one restoration-performance metric (outplant survival, growth, or thermal tolerance).

Bridges to: D2 (reef ecosystems and living coasts).

2

Living Coasts: Ecosystems, Organisms, and Human Health

MBE · EVR · OCE

Marine ecology, population genomics, organismal physiology, and the human-environment links that connect coastal ecosystems to community well-being (MBE, EVR, OCE). These four projects pair field or observational sampling with controlled genomic, physiological, isotopic, or biometric characterization, then interpret it in an ecological, organismal, or human-health frame.

D. Crawford & M. Oleksiak MBE

Population genomics of fish or invertebrate species in Biscayne Bay and adjacent south Florida waters, with a 10-week workflow spanning DNA extraction, sequencing preparation, and computational analysis. The student analyzes one environmental contrast for a defined organism and marker set; if wet-lab work is delayed, archived sequence data on the same contrast provide a fallback.

Draws on: the Modeling, AI & Technology layer (computational genomics, bioinformatics).

K. Ashad-Bishop EVR

Environmental-health research on urban heat, coastal-resource access, and heat-related health risk in Miami-Dade County. The student analyzes existing de-identified urban-heat and resource-access data under the mentor's approved IRB protocol, with no new human-subjects recruitment, for example relating extreme-heat exposure to healthcare access.

Bridges to: D1 (extreme heat as hazard and climate extreme).

R. Heuer MBE

Effects of climate-related stressors and environmental pollutants on neural and behavioral function in Aplysia or zebrafish, using behavioral assays, imaging, and physiology. The student runs one focused experimental comparison (stressor versus control on a single endpoint), with an existing assay dataset as a fallback.

Bridges to: D1 (climate stressors) and D3 (pollutant biogeochemistry).

H. Close OCE

Organic and isotopic analyses of marine particulate matter, sediments, or organism tissues to investigate carbon and nitrogen sourcing and food-web structure within the ocean biological pump. The student analyzes a defined sample subset addressing one focused isotopic or compositional question.

Bridges to: D3 (biological pump and biogeochemical cycling).

3

Coastal Change and Earth-System Cycles

OCE · MGS · MBE · ATM

Biogeochemical cycling, microbial food webs, and the sediment, genomic, and model-based records that reconstruct long-term environmental change (OCE, MGS, MBE, ATM). These six projects reduce a defined material or model, whether a sediment core, isotopic sample, metagenome, autonomous float record, or simulation, to a focused measurement and interpret it within a longer environmental or Earth-system context.

M. Bif OCE

Ocean carbon and nitrogen cycling investigated using BGC-Argo floats, satellite remote sensing, and shipboard observations. The student integrates float, satellite, and ship data for one target region and quantifies one rate such as net primary production, carbon export, or remineralization.

Bridges to: D2 (coastal ecosystem productivity); draws on the Modeling, AI & Technology layer (autonomous floats, remote sensing).

G. Eberli, A. Oehlert, R. Weger MGS

Analysis of a high-recovery Last-Glacial-Maximum fringing-reef sediment-core archive offshore Mozambique. The student owns one core interval from petrophysical, mineralogical, or geochemical measurement to its placement on the deglacial sea-level curve, interpreting its implication for the rate of sea-level rise.

Bridges to: D1 (paleo record of past sea-level hazard).

A. Holt MGS

Computational geodynamics of active plate margins and their role in long-term coastal uplift and hazard. The student uses global plate-tectonic reconstruction tools to identify double-subduction settings and tests plate-speed scaling relationships in Python against published numerical models.

Bridges to: D1 (long-term tectonic and coastal hazard).

M. Moniruzzaman MBE

Giant-virus impacts on coastal microbial food webs and biogeochemistry, using field sampling, metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, and bioinformatics. The student identifies and annotates a defined viral marker group across one environmental contrast.

Bridges to: D2 (microbial ecology, coastal food webs); draws on the Modeling, AI & Technology layer (metagenomic and bioinformatic workflows).

E. Babcock MBE

Quantitative marine ecology applied to fisheries and ecosystem management (e.g., protected-species bycatch, species-distribution modeling, food-web response to nutrient pollution in Biscayne Bay, or seagrass-restoration value for juvenile gag grouper). The student pairs with a Babcock-lab PhD co-mentor on one defined sub-question and conducts analyses in R with version-controlled, AI-assisted workflows; no prior quantitative experience required.

Draws on: the Modeling, AI & Technology layer; connects to D2 (ecosystem management).

S. Wang ATM

A machine-learning emulator of an aerosol chemistry model, built to run far faster than the full model within atmospheric-modeling workflows. The student trains and benchmarks an emulator over a defined aerosol-composition range against the reference model.

Anchors the Modeling, AI & Technology layer; connects to D1 (aerosol-climate coupling).

Modeling, AI, and Technology cross-cutting layer

Runs across all three domains · ATM · MGS · OCE · MBE

Computational modeling, machine learning, AI-assisted analysis, and observing-system technology run through all three domains rather than forming a domain of their own. They supply the shared tools each domain uses to observe, quantify, and interpret, and are themselves a source of scientific contributions. Every student works in reproducible, version-controlled, AI-assisted workflows regardless of domain, and weekly cross-project discussions and a co-authored Methods Compendium make these shared methods explicit across the cohort.

Projects in which these methods are central include explainable-AI marine-heatwave prediction (Arcodia, Domain 1), dense-node seismic signal processing (Lin, Domain 1), a machine-learning aerosol-model emulator (Wang, Domain 3), quantitative fisheries and ecosystem modeling (Babcock, Domain 3), and autonomous BGC-Argo float data science (Bif, Domain 3).
