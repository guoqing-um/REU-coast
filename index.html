About COAST

South Florida sits at the leading edge of coupled coastal Earth-system change: intensifying tropical cyclones, marine heatwaves, accelerating sea-level rise, reef decline, and coastal solid-Earth responses to ocean and storm forcing. COAST uses this region and the broader tropical western Atlantic as a living laboratory for undergraduate research that crosses traditional disciplinary boundaries.

The Rosenstiel School is one of very few places in the United States where atmospheric, ocean, marine-biology, environmental, and geoscience faculty work side by side on Virginia Key. The school houses the NOAA Cooperative Institute for Marine and Atmospheric Studies (CIMAS), sits directly across the street from NOAA's Atlantic Oceanographic and Meteorological Laboratory (AOML), and is near the U.S. National Hurricane Center. COAST embeds students in this research environment for a full ten weeks of mentored, hands-on coastal Earth-system science.

Ocean

Currents, chemistry, and life at sea

Physical oceanography, ocean biogeochemistry and carbon cycling, marine ecology and population biology, and autonomous and ship-based observing across the coastal ocean.

Atmosphere

Weather, climate, and air-sea exchange

Boundary-layer dynamics, aerosol chemistry, climate modeling, and machine-learning approaches to weather and climate, including the storms that shape South Florida.

Solid Earth

Coasts, sediments, and the seafloor

Seismology and geophysical imaging, carbonate sedimentology, paleoclimate and sea-level reconstruction, and the coupling of ocean and storm forcing to the solid Earth.

Technology

Sensing & computing infrastructure

The R/V F. G. Walton Smith, the SUSTAIN hurricane-scale wind-wave-surge laboratory, the CSTARS remote-sensing facility, genomics infrastructure, AI and modeling workflows, and HPC through UM's Frost Institute for Data Science and Computing.

Participating Departments

COAST projects span all five departments of the Rosenstiel School, embedding each cohort in a school-wide interdisciplinary research environment:

ATM
Atmospheric Sciences
Boundary-layer dynamics, aerosols, climate modeling, ML for weather
EVR
Environmental Science & Policy
Climate risk, hazard assessment, adaptation, decision support
MBE
Marine Biology & Ecology
Coral reefs, fisheries, population genomics, microbial ecology
MGS
Marine Geosciences
Seismology, sedimentology, paleoclimate, geodynamics
OCE
Ocean Sciences
Physical oceanography, biogeochemistry, isotope geochemistry, observing systems

Sixteen projects across four themes

Each cohort is matched to a rotating set of projects spanning the coupled coastal Earth system, from hurricanes and heatwaves to coral reefs, ocean biogeochemistry, and AI-driven prediction.
