Student Life & Logistics

A fully supported summer in Miami

Stipend, housing, and round-trip travel are all covered. There is no cost to participate.

Full Support, No Cost to Participate

COAST leases the housing centrally, so no one has to find a place or front the cost, and the weekly stipend is set to cover day-to-day living costs including meals and daily transit.

$700/week NSF stipend, paid on a regular schedule across all ten weeks.
Housing arranged Leased and paid by the program for the full ten weeks.
Travel covered Round-trip travel to and from Miami at the start and end of the program.

What to Expect

Stipend

Because the stipend covers meals and daily transit, there is no separate meal allowance to apply for.

Housing

Rosenstiel occupies its own island campus on Virginia Key, which has essentially no residential housing, so the cohort is housed together in shared near-campus units on the mainland or Key Biscayne, along the Metrobus Route 26 corridor.

Getting Around

Route 26 runs directly to campus, a short ride that also serves early and late working hours, and daily transit is covered by the stipend. Transport for all group field activities is arranged by the program.

Field Activities

Four half-days through the summer: the Rosenstiel and CIMAS campus tour, the SUSTAIN hurricane-scale laboratory, an Everglades and coastal-observing day, and the R/V F. G. Walton Smith.

Research Facilities

Depending on your project: the research vessel, the SUSTAIN laboratory, coral and live-organism wet-labs, seismic arrays, genomics and sequencing, and high-performance computing through UM's Frost Institute.

Beyond the Summer

Career programming spans academic, NOAA, industry, non-profit, and applied-science routes. Mentor support continues into the academic year toward conference presentations, manuscripts, theses, and graduate applications.

Living in Miami

The Rosenstiel campus sits between Biscayne Bay and the Atlantic, a short causeway ride from downtown Miami and Miami Beach. Summer here is warm and tropical, with afternoon sea breezes, dramatic thunderstorms, and the rhythms of the hurricane season, so students live inside the coastal Earth system they study. Miami is one of the most international coastal cities in the United States, with a vibrant arts, music, and food scene.

If you are coming from a cooler region, expect it to be hotter and more humid than you are used to. Laboratory and cohort spaces are air-conditioned, field activities are scheduled for early morning or late afternoon, the program provides cooling and sun-protection gear, and heat-safety training is part of Week-1 orientation.

Safe, respectful, and harassment-free environment

COAST maintains a safe, respectful, and harassment-free environment for all participants, under a written code of conduct reviewed during orientation week alongside University of Miami policies and reporting procedures. Off-campus research follows the program's fieldwork safety plan. Accommodations for students with disabilities are arranged through the UM Office of Disability Services, and students always have support pathways outside their own research group.
