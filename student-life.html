Full Support, No Cost to Participate

COAST is designed as a fully supported in-person experience. Stipend, housing, and round-trip travel to Miami are all covered, and the weekly stipend is set to cover day-to-day living costs, including meals. Students live together in shared housing near the Rosenstiel campus on Virginia Key, keeping them adjacent to their laboratories and field sites with no daily inter-campus commute.

What to Expect

Stipend & Support

Participants receive the NSF stipend of $700 per week across all ten weeks of the program, paid on a regular schedule. The stipend is set to cover day-to-day living costs, including meals, and round-trip travel to and from Miami is reimbursed. There is no cost to participate.

Housing

Shared cohort housing is provided near the Rosenstiel campus on Virginia Key for the full ten weeks, keeping students close to their labs and field sites. Living together strengthens cohort cohesion and removes financial and logistical barriers for students from outside the Miami area.

Getting Around

Housing near Virginia Key keeps you within easy reach of the Rosenstiel campus, with no daily inter-campus commute. Cohort field activities (CSTARS, the R/V Walton Smith, the National Hurricane Center, the Florida Reef Tract, and the Everglades) are arranged and transported by the program.

Research Environment

You'll have access to the R/V F. G. Walton Smith, the SUSTAIN hurricane-scale wind-wave-surge laboratory, CSTARS remote sensing, genomics and sequencing facilities, and high-performance computing through UM's Frost Institute for Data Science and Computing.

Cohort Programming

Weekly research seminars, cross-project integration discussions, skills workshops (Python/Jupyter, version control, scientific writing, oral presentation), Responsible Conduct of Research training, career-pathways panels, and a culminating end-of-summer public symposium.

Career Pathways

Career panels span academia, NOAA and other federal laboratories, industry, non-profit, and applied-science paths. Selected students are supported to present at national meetings (AGU, AMS, Ocean Sciences, ASLO, SACNAS) and to continue research with their mentor beyond the summer.

Living in Miami

The Rosenstiel campus on Virginia Key sits between Biscayne Bay and the Atlantic, a short causeway ride from downtown Miami and Miami Beach. Summer in South Florida is hot, humid, and shaped by sea-breeze convection and the seasonal threat of tropical cyclones; students experience first-hand the demanding environmental conditions they study. Miami is one of the most international coastal cities in the United States, with rich Cuban, Haitian, Caribbean, Central American, and South American communities and a vibrant arts, music, and food scene.

Safe, Respectful, and Inclusive Environment

COAST is committed to a safe, respectful, inclusive, and harassment-free environment for all participants. Orientation week includes review of UM policies and reporting procedures, expectations of professional conduct, and field-safety planning. The Site Coordinator works with the UM Office of Disability Services to provide individualized accommodations for students with disabilities, including accessible housing, transportation, and lab and field accommodations.
